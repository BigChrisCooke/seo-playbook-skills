# Evidence-backed content patterns

Use these structures only where they help the reader. Bracketed fields are
unfilled templates, not real quotations, studies, measurements, or facts.

## Claim-source ledger

| Claim | Primary source URL | Source date and measured period | Checked date | Scope and caveats | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact draft claim | Page actually opened | Keep publication date separate from data period | Actual verification date | Platform, sample, geography, methodology | Keep / qualify / correct / remove |

An inaccessible source remains unverified. A confident citation in another AI
answer is a lead to investigate, not evidence that the source exists.

## Statistic

Template: `[Organization]'s [linked source] reports [measured result] for
[population/platform] during [period]. [Methodological limitation].`

Include the source only after reading it. Prefer the original dataset, paper,
documentation, or report. State whether a number is observed, derived, or an
estimate. Explain calculations and do not generalize a study's result into a
guaranteed visibility lift.

## Quotation

Template: `[Speaker] said [short exact quotation], in [linked primary source,
date and context].`

Verify wording and attribution. If the original cannot be found, paraphrase
only supported information or omit the quote. Never invent a quote from a
recognizable expert to make a worked example sound authoritative.

## Answer, comparison, or procedure

Answer the actual reader question, include the verified differentiators or
steps, then cite the evidence needed to assess the claim. Use an accessible
comparison table when the options share meaningful fields. Preserve product
links and the intended next action.

There is no universal word count, answer-block length, markup type, or
formatting change that guarantees selection by an AI system.
